# Targeted Job Postings — Format + Draft Examples

**Status:** Draft for team review. Implements Pillar C (BRIEF §5) in the "craft at speed" voice.

The whole point (per Eric): a role here should read like **"here's the actual hard problem you'd own,"** not a generic responsibilities list. It's a recruiting tool, not a careers-page mirror.

---

## The format

Every role uses the same five beats:

1. **The domain** — what corner of Deel you'd live in, in one honest sentence.
2. **The problem you'd own** — the specific, hard, real thing. Named. Not "improve the user experience."
3. **What good looks like in 6–12 months** — a concrete picture of success.
4. **Who you'd work with** — the team, the partners, the pace.
5. **What we're looking for** — signal, not a checklist of years.

Then a link to a relevant **Story** (so the problem has a human face) and the **apply** action.

## Example — Product Designer, Global Payroll

**The domain.** Payroll is the part of Deel people feel most viscerally — it's their money, on time, correct, in their currency. You'd own design for the surfaces that run it across dozens of countries.

**The problem you'd own.** Running payroll today means a payroll admin reconciling edge cases across jurisdictions with rules that contradict each other. Your job: make the correct action the obvious one, and make the scary moments (a mismatch, a deadline, a compliance flag) calm instead of terrifying — without dumbing down the real complexity underneath.

**What good looks like in 6–12 months.** Admins run a multi-country cycle with visible confidence; error-recovery stops being a support ticket and becomes a self-serve moment; the flows you designed hold up as we add more countries without a redesign each time.

**Who you'd work with.** A tight pod with PM and eng who know the domain cold, plus payroll ops who've seen every edge case. Fast cycles — you'll see your work live in weeks, not quarters.

**What we're looking for.** You get energized, not scared, by genuine complexity. You've designed for expert users doing high-stakes work. You have opinions about defaults. You can move fast without shipping sloppy.

## Example — Senior Product Designer, Contractor Experience

**The domain.** Millions of contractors get paid through Deel. You'd own the experience for the person on the receiving end — often a freelancer in a country far from where the money starts.

**The problem you'd own.** A contractor's core anxiety is simple: *when do I get paid, how much, and why is it different this time?* Behind that question is a mess of currencies, fees, withdrawal methods, and timing. Your job is to make that anxiety disappear — to make "where's my money" a question people stop needing to ask.

**What good looks like in 6–12 months.** Contractors understand their payout before they have to ask support; withdrawal setup stops being a drop-off point; trust metrics (and the absence of "where's my money" tickets) move.

**Who you'd work with.** Payments eng, a PM who lives in the funnel data, and a support team who'll tell you exactly where trust breaks. Global user base — you'll design for people you'll never share a timezone with.

**What we're looking for.** You care about the anxious, non-expert user. You've untangled money/timing/status flows before. You can hold "make it feel simple" and "the reality is complicated" in your head at once.
