# Deel.design — Editorial Positioning (rethink)

**Status:** Proposal for discussion — supersedes the "two-tier voice" framing in BRIEF.md §4
**Date:** 2026-07-05

---

## The problem with "two tiers"

The original brief organized the site around a split between **polished vision statements** and **scrappy designer stories**. That split describes *how much effort we spend producing something* — an internal ops concern. It is not a reason for anyone to read the site, and it makes the site feel like two things stapled together.

The audience (prospective designers) doesn't care about our production tiers. They care about one question: **is this a place where good design happens and good designers grow?**

## The fix: one spine, one voice

Organize everything around a single editorial point of view that answers that question:

> ### Craft at speed
> Design at Deel is real craft done inside extreme velocity and genuine domain complexity — payroll, compliance, global employment. The interesting, honest, proud-making thing about designing here is that the craft *survives the speed*. That's what we show.

Everything ladders to it:

- **Principles** — our opinions about *how you keep craft alive when you ship fast.*
- **Stories** — designers showing *craft under pressure*: a specific hard problem, solved fast, done well.
- **Roles** — *here is the hard, specific problem you'd own*, at this pace.

## What happens to the "two tiers"

They stop being the structure and become a single internal production guideline:

> **Production note (internal):** Keep declarative statements (principles, vision) considered and few. Keep stories honest and low-production — a lightly-edited conversation beats a labored essay. Never let production value become a distraction from product work. *(This is Eric's and Travis's caution — kept as an operating rule, not an organizing idea.)*

## Voice, in one paragraph

Confident but unpretentious. We're proud of the work without being precious about it. Specific over sweeping — a real screen, a real number, a real tradeoff beats an adjective. We don't do corporate design thought leadership; we show the work and let it talk. Written like a good designer talks to another good designer they respect.

## What this changes downstream

- **IA** (BRIEF §6) is unchanged — Principles / Stories / Roles still hold. Only the *connective tissue* changes: every page reads as one voice on one theme.
- **Home** leads with the "craft at speed" idea, not a generic vision statement.
- **Success** is still recruiting-led (BRIEF §11).
