# Deel.design — Website Brief (v1)

**Status:** Draft for team review
**Date:** 2026-07-05
**Owner:** Design team
**Contributors referenced:** Eric, Travis

---

## 1. In one line

A public-facing home for Deel Design that makes talented designers want to work here — by showing the real craft, real people, and real problems behind the product, not corporate thought leadership.

---

## 2. Why we're building this

Two goals, in priority order:

1. **Attract talent.** Give prospective designers a genuine, high-signal look at what it's like to design at Deel — the problems, the pace, the people — and a low-friction path from "that looks interesting" to "I applied."
2. **Build internal pride & retention.** Give the current team a place they're proud to point to. Publishing our own work and stories is itself a retention lever: it signals the work matters and gets seen.

These reinforce each other. The same authentic stories that make current designers proud are what make outside designers want in.

> **Non-goal (for now):** This is *not* a marketing/demand-gen site, a design-system reference for external consumption, or a channel for executive thought leadership. See §9.

---

## 3. Audience

| Audience | What they want | What we give them |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospective designers** (primary) | Is this a place where good design happens and good designers grow? | Honest stories, visible craft, roles that name the actual problem |
| **Current Deel designers** (primary) | Something to be proud of; a place my work lives | Their work and voice, published with a high bar |
| **Peers / the design community** (secondary) | Does Deel take design seriously? | First-principles brand thinking, eventually the design system |
| **Cross-functional partners & candidates' networks** (tertiary) | Context, credibility | Clear, well-produced vision statements |

**Primary journey to optimize:** prospective designer lands → reads 1–2 designer stories → sees a role whose description actually describes the *work* → applies.

---

## 4. Positioning & voice — the two-tier principle

The single most important editorial decision in this brief:

- **Tier 1 — Declarative vision (high bar, low volume).** Brand first principles and vision statements are polished, considered, and rarely changed. These are the load-bearing statements. Quality over quantity.
- **Tier 2 — On-the-ground stories (casual, scrappy, higher volume).** Designer stories are intentionally low-production: honest, specific, human. A designer talking through a problem they solved, what they learned at Deel's speed, a quick interview about a senior→staff promotion. Polishing these to death would kill the thing that makes them credible.

Getting this split right is what keeps the site authentic *and* prevents it from becoming a production sink (see §8).

---

## 5. Content pillars

### Pillar A — Brand & Design Systems
- **Now:** Publish our **brand first principles** — the beliefs that guide how we design. Small, sharp, declarative.
- **Later (~6 months):** The **design system**, presented as a point of view, not just a component dump. Reference points: **Google** (Material as a articulated philosophy) and **Wise** (opinionated, brand-forward system site).
- *Readiness note:* Per Eric, we are **not ready** to publish the full design system yet. Publishing it half-baked undercuts the bigger story. Plant the seed now (first principles), tell the full story when it's ready.

### Pillar B — "On the Ground" Designer Stories
- The heart of the site. Authentic, individual, scrappy.
- Formats:
  - **"A problem I solved"** — one designer, one concrete problem, what they shipped and why.
  - **"What I learned designing at speed"** — reflections on working at Deel's intensity.
  - **"Senior → Staff"** — casual interview on how someone leveled up.
- Deliberately low-production. Voice > polish.

### Pillar C — Targeted Job Postings
- Roles that go **beyond the standard JD** (per Eric): name the **domain and the specific goals** the candidate would own.
- Each role reads like "here's the actual problem you'd be tackling," making the site a genuine recruiting tool rather than a careers-page mirror.
- Tight link from stories → relevant open roles.

---

## 6. Information architecture (proposed)

```
Home                     — vision statement + featured story + featured role
├── Principles           — brand first principles (Tier 1, Pillar A)
├── Stories              — designer stories index (Tier 2, Pillar B)
│   └── /[story]
├── Work                 — selected work / case snapshots (optional, phase 2)
├── Open Roles           — targeted job postings (Pillar C)
│   └── /[role]
└── (Later) Design System — full system, ~6 months out (Pillar A)
```

Home does the recruiting job in one screen: **who we are (vision) + proof (a story) + a door (a role).**

## 7. Phased roadmap

Anchored to Eric's "planting seeds now, bigger story in ~6 months" framing.

| Phase | Timing | Ship | Explicitly *not* yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0 — Seed** | Now → launch | Home, Principles (first principles only), 3–5 designer stories, targeted Open Roles | Full design system, leadership essays |
| **Phase 1 — Prove** | Post-launch | Steady trickle of stories (batched, not on a treadmill), more roles as they open | — |
| **Phase 2 — Tell the big story** | ~6 months | Design system as a point of view; deeper work showcases | — |

The bar for launch is **quality of a small set**, not breadth.

## 8. Content operations — avoiding the treadmill

Directly addressing Travis's caution ("no one's waiting for corporate design thought leadership") and Eric's warning against the site becoming a high-production distraction:

- **No newsletter, no fixed publishing cadence, no commitment to a constant stream.** The site must not create an obligation the team resents.
- **Evergreen-first.** Content should be worth reading a year from now. No news, no dated commentary.
- **Batch production.** Capture 3–5 stories in a short push, publish over time. Avoid the "what do we post this week" trap.
- **Lightweight capture for Tier 2.** A recorded 20-minute conversation lightly edited beats a written essay nobody has time to write. Lower the cost of a story to near-zero.
- **A high, non-negotiable bar for Tier 1.** The few declarative statements get real care.

Rule of thumb: **if a format would require someone to produce content on a schedule, we don't commit to it.**

---

## 9. Scope guardrails — what this is NOT

- ❌ Not the full design system (yet).
- ❌ Not executive/leadership thought leadership.
- ❌ Not a newsletter or any cadence-based content commitment.
- ❌ Not a high-production showcase that drains the team's energy from actual product work.
- ❌ Not a duplicate of the corporate careers page — roles here are richer and domain-specific.

---

## 10. Design direction

- **Foundation:** Build on the **Deel design system** for speed, consistency, and quality — but push it to be **more brandable** and expressive than the product UI. The product DS optimizes for utility; this site should feel like it has a point of view.
- **Levers to make it "more brandable":** distinct typographic voice, more editorial layouts, a warmer/bolder color expression on top of DS tokens, generous use of real design artifacts (screens, sketches, process shots) as imagery.
- **Story pages should feel human and casual**; principle pages should feel considered and declarative — the visual system should support the two-tier voice from §4.

---

## 11. Success metrics

- **Primary:** Qualified inbound applications attributed to the site; quality of applicants ("they got what we're about").
- **Recruiting funnel:** story → role click-through → apply rate.
- **Internal:** team sentiment / pride (light pulse), designers volunteering to contribute stories.
- **Not a metric:** raw traffic or publishing volume. We're not chasing an audience.
